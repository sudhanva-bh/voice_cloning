\begin{abstract}
On-device zero-shot neural voice cloning enables the immediate replication of an unseen human speaker's acoustic timbre and prosody from a brief reference recording (three to five seconds) without model retraining or parameter fine-tuning. While modern continuous flow-matching models demonstrate remarkable expressive fidelity on cloud graphics accelerators, deploying these models on resource-constrained edge hardware presents formidable algorithmic, numerical, and systems-level hurdles. Specifically, low-tier commodity platforms such as the Lenovo Tab K10 (MediaTek Helio P22T with 4\,GB RAM) lack dedicated neural processing units, possess constrained memory bandwidth, and enforce strict user-space memory limits before triggering the Android operating system's \textit{LowMemoryKiller}. Furthermore, existing lightweight edge speech engines exclusively target English and Mandarin phonology, leaving a critical research gap for Dravidian and Indo-Aryan languages characterized by complex syllabic structures (\textit{aksharas}), consonant conjuncts, and vowel ligatures.

This treatise presents the design, mathematical formulation, empirical benchmarking, and hardware optimization of a dual-engine on-device multilingual voice cloning architecture. For English synthesis, a non-autoregressive Zipformer backbone (ZipVoice-Distill, 123M parameters, INT8 quantized to 104\,MB) is coupled with a 24\,kHz Vocos vocoder and executed via Sherpa-ONNX. For native Indian languages, we formulate and verify an Indic voice cloning pipeline based on a 22-layer Diffusion Transformer (IndicF5, 335M parameters) trained on 1,417 hours of multi-speaker speech across eleven languages. We resolve critical duration mismatch artifacts by developing a calibrated syllabic akshara duration model (yielding 6.5 mel frames per character), and establish the spatial text-mel alignment law governing in-context conditioning. Physical hardware profiling across Samsung Galaxy S23, Galaxy Tab S9 FE+, and Galaxy M53 reveals real-time factor scaling and peak resident set size dynamics, validating our static INT8 quantization (349\,MB DiT with 22\,MB Vocos) within an 801\,MB operational memory envelope. Finally, we formulate a comprehensive production migration roadmap to Google LiteRT, leveraging the unified \textit{CompiledModel} API, Qualcomm AI Engine Direct (QNN), MediaTek NeuroPilot, and consistency distillation to achieve deterministic, real-time edge synthesis.
\end{abstract}
