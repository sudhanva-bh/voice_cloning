\begin{abstract}
{\sloppy
On-device zero-shot neural voice cloning enables the localized replication of an unseen human speaker's acoustic timbre and prosody from a brief reference recording (three to five seconds) without model fine-tuning or server communication. This report investigates this capability specifically to empower government school children and visually impaired (blind) students with accessible audio learning in their own classroom teacher's voice. In rural and semi-urban government schools, foundational learners demonstrate significantly higher cognitive engagement, retention, and comfort when educational content is articulated in the familiar, trusted voice of their local teacher rather than an unfamiliar or accent-mismatched synthetic voice. For visually impaired students, who rely entirely on auditory channels for textbook study and concept explanations, hearing lessons in their teacher's voice provides vital pedagogical continuity and confidence. 

Despite its societal importance, this educational domain remains largely untapped by commercial speech synthesis, which prioritizes cloud entertainment and Western languages. Deploying voice cloning in government school settings introduces severe operational constraints: classrooms frequently operate in low-connectivity rural zones with zero internet access, rely on low-cost government-procured tablets such as the Lenovo Tab K10 (MediaTek Helio P22T with 4\,GB RAM), and have zero budget for cloud API fees. Furthermore, the system must enforce absolute biometric privacy for teachers and children while operating within strict physical memory limits before the Linux kernel's \textit{LowMemoryKiller} terminates user processes.

This report develops an offline, multilingual zero-shot voice cloning pipeline designed for commodity Android hardware. We implement a dual-engine architecture consisting of: (i) an optimized English baseline using a non-autoregressive Zipformer backbone (ZipVoice-Distill, 123M parameters, INT8 quantized to 104\,MB) with a 24\,kHz Vocos vocoder executed via Sherpa-ONNX; and (ii) an Indic voice cloning engine based on a 22-layer Diffusion Transformer (IndicF5, 335M parameters) adapted from AI4Bharat's 1,417-hour multilingual foundation model. To satisfy edge memory budgets, we apply static INT8 quantization and pre-allocated memory arenas, reducing the IndicF5 DiT from 1.35\,GB down to 349\,MB and the Vocos vocoder to 22\,MB, bounding peak resident memory to 801\,MB. We formulate a calibrated on-device syllabic duration model (6.5 mel frames per character) that eliminates grapheme-to-phoneme rule dictionaries, and engineer a native C++ runtime engine for mobile execution. Physical hardware profiling across four ARM device tiers validates process stability without out-of-memory terminations. Finally, we formulate a concrete production roadmap targeting Google LiteRT and hardware neural processing units (Qualcomm QNN HTP and MediaTek NeuroPilot), establishing that high-fidelity multilingual voice cloning can be achieved entirely at the edge to support accessible education.
\par}
\end{abstract}
