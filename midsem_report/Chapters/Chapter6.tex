\section{Summary of Research Accomplishments and Milestones}
This mid-semester investigation developed, evaluated, and optimized an on-device, zero-shot multilingual neural voice cloning pipeline capable of operating entirely offline on resource-constrained Android mobile hardware. Addressing the lack of Indian language support in edge speech processing, where existing zero-shot engines exclusively target English and Chinese, this project brings native Indian language voice synthesis to commodity mobile platforms.

The primary engineering milestones accomplished during this reporting period include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Mobile Proof of Concept (PoC)}: Developed and benchmarked the mobile feasibility proof of concept using the ZipVoice-Distill 123M flow-matching model and 24\,kHz Vocos vocoder within a Flutter application (\texorpdfstring{\texttt{voice\_clone\_proto}}{voice\_clone\_proto}), demonstrating physical memory viability and discovering the Fixed Overhead Anomaly and 1.5\,GB memory cliff that guided our Indic implementation.
    \item \textbf{Verification of Native Indic Voice Cloning}: Ported and adapted AI4Bharat's 335M Diffusion Transformer (IndicF5) across Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and Hindi, verifying acoustic speaker adaptation without requiring external grapheme-to-phoneme rule dictionaries.
    \item \textbf{Formulation of the Syllabic Akshara Duration Model}: Identified and resolved the duration mismatch defect that caused trailing silence and repeated syllables, establishing a calibrated formulation of \textbf{6.5 mel frames per Unicode character} ($\approx 55$\,ms per character).
    \item \textbf{Identification of the Reference Duration Boundary}: Characterized the sequence alignment constraints governing ConvNeXt text representations, demonstrating that reference audio clips must be bounded strictly between \textbf{3.5 and 5.0 seconds} to avoid positional attention collapse.
    \item \textbf{In-Memory Batch Benchmarking Engine and Dataset}: Engineered \texttt{batch.py} with warm RAM model caching and curated \texttt{prompts.json}, comprising \textbf{120 authentic conversational sentences} across Tamil, Telugu, and Hindi stratified across four duration tiers.
    \item \textbf{Full Model Integer Quantization}: Exported and verified the static INT8 ONNX pipeline, achieving a \textbf{74\% reduction in model size} (from 1.35\,GB FP32 down to 349\,MB) for the DiT backbone and quantizing the Vocos vocoder down to 22\,MB.
    \item \textbf{Memory Budget Safety on Edge Devices}: Confirmed that the quantized system bounds operational physical memory to \textbf{801\,MB}, safely operating below the 1.5\,GB \textit{LowMemoryKiller} threshold on 4\,GB edge devices like the Lenovo Tab K10.
    \item \textbf{Formulation of the Google LiteRT Roadmap}: Established the post-midsem roadmap toward Google LiteRT, leveraging the compiled model API to offload matrix operations to Qualcomm Hexagon and MediaTek NPUs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key Scientific, Systems, and Societal Contributions}
The technical and humanitarian contributions of this project span three primary dimensions:

\subsection{Theoretical and Linguistic Contributions}
By connecting continuous flow matching with the syllabic structure of Dravidian and Indo-Aryan scripts, this research provides an empirical characterization of akshara frame density for flow-matching TTS. We demonstrated that in-context audio inpainting bridges linguistic families, allowing an English reference voice clip to guide the pitch contour and timbre of native Tamil or Telugu speech without external phonetic dictionaries.

\subsection{Systems and Edge Deployment Contributions}
Through in-kernel physical memory profiling (\texttt{/proc/self/status}) across physical silicon tiers, this research demonstrated the empirical reality of the Fixed Overhead Anomaly and established rigorous memory budgets for edge generative AI. We showed that by pairing static INT8 tensor graphs with Fourier-based Inverse STFT neural vocoders (Vocos), deep diffusion models can execute deterministically within the strict memory and compute boundaries of budget edge platforms, safely bounded within 801\,MB.

\subsection{Pedagogical and Accessibility Impact in Untapped Domains}
Commercial speech synthesis platforms have predominantly focused on entertainment, commercial assistants, and Western languages, leaving foundational education in vernacular public schools and special education for visually impaired learners almost completely untapped. This investigation demonstrates how on-device voice cloning addresses these critical societal needs:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Familiar-Voice Learning for Government School Children}: Primary school children in rural and semi-urban government schools exhibit higher listening comprehension and emotional comfort when instruction is delivered in the familiar voice of their local teacher.
    \item \textbf{Accessible Auditory Learning for Blind Students}: For visually impaired students who rely entirely on auditory channels for academic study, hearing textbook lessons, practice questions, and conceptual explanations articulated in their teacher's voice provides essential pedagogical continuity and cognitive trust.
    \item \textbf{Economic and Operational Feasibility}: Operating entirely offline on state-subsidized tablets (such as the Lenovo Tab K10) with zero recurring cloud API fees ensures that schools can generate unlimited audio educational aids without fiscal constraints, while protecting the biometric voice privacy of educators and children.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Concluding Remarks and Immediate Priorities}
The work completed during this phase demonstrates that zero-shot multilingual voice cloning can execute fully offline on budget commodity hardware, without transmitting user biometric voice data to remote servers. By tailoring this capability to the untapped domains of vernacular government schools and accessible education for visually impaired students, this project provides a practical pathway for personalized, familiar-voice audio learning at zero marginal infrastructure cost. The technical feasibility of this approach has been substantiated through mathematical formulation, ten consecutive engineering iterations, and physical hardware profiling across four distinct silicon tiers.

The immediate priorities for the post-midsem phase are ordered as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Google LiteRT Migration}: Execute the \texttt{litert-torch} PyTorch-to-FlatBuffer conversion and validate numerical parity (MCD $<$ 3.5\,dB) against the PyTorch FP32 baseline.
    \item \textbf{NPU Hardware Delegation}: Profile and verify 100\% DiT operator offloading to Qualcomm QNN HTP (Galaxy S23) and MediaTek NeuroPilot (Galaxy M53).
    \item \textbf{2-Step Consistency Distillation}: Apply FlashSpeech consistency distillation to reduce the ODE solver from 8 steps to 2 steps, targeting sub-2$\times$ RTF on budget CPU hardware.
    \item \textbf{Lenovo Tab K10 Benchmarking}: Conduct on-device memory profiling under sustained classroom lesson generation to guarantee RSS $<$ 850\,MB and zero LMK terminations.
    \item \textbf{Classroom Accessibility and Flutter UI Integration}: Wire the native IndicF5 Dart FFI service into the Flutter application, building on the mobile PoC architecture while integrating large-target controls and TalkBack accessibility features for pilot evaluations with educators and visually impaired students.
\end{enumerate}
