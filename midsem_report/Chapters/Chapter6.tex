\section{Summary of Research Accomplishments and Milestones}
This mid-semester investigation developed, evaluated, and optimized an on-device, zero-shot multilingual neural voice cloning pipeline capable of operating entirely offline on resource-constrained Android mobile hardware. Addressing the lack of Indian language support in edge speech processing, where existing zero-shot engines predominantly target English and Chinese, this project investigates bringing native Indian language voice synthesis to commodity mobile platforms.

The primary engineering milestones accomplished during this reporting period include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Mobile Proof of Concept (PoC)}: Developed and benchmarked the mobile feasibility proof of concept using the ZipVoice-Distill 123M flow-matching model and 24\,kHz Vocos vocoder within a Flutter application (\texorpdfstring{\texttt{voice\_clone\_proto}}{voice\_clone\_proto}). On the target Lenovo Tab K10 (MediaTek Helio P22T, 4\,GB RAM), ZipVoice completed all nine trials across short, medium, and long tiers with zero LowMemoryKiller terminations, exhibiting RTF from $8.97\times$ to $19.99\times$ and post-utterance resident memory up to 2,491\,MB. These physical measurements refuted the hypothesis of a universal 1,500\,MB crash ceiling while demonstrating memory variability that motivated deterministic static arena design for IndicF5.
    \item \textbf{Verification of Native Indic Voice Cloning}: Ported and adapted AI4Bharat's 335M Diffusion Transformer (IndicF5) across Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and Hindi, verifying acoustic speaker adaptation without requiring external grapheme-to-phoneme rule dictionaries.
    \item \textbf{Formulation of the Syllabic Duration Model}: Identified and resolved the duration mismatch defect that caused trailing silence and repeated syllables, establishing a calibrated formulation of \textbf{6.5 mel frames per Unicode character} ($\approx 69.33$\,ms per character code point, yielding $\approx 111\text{--}153$\,ms per spoken akshara).
    \item \textbf{Calibration of Reference Audio Duration}: Characterized the sequence alignment constraints governing ConvNeXt text representations and bidirectional self-attention, establishing a practical recommendation of \textbf{3.5 to 5.0 seconds} for reference clips to maintain spatial conditioning and avoid attention dispersion.
    \item \textbf{In-Memory Batch Benchmarking Engine and Dataset}: Engineered \texttt{batch.py} with warm RAM model caching and curated \texttt{prompts.json}, comprising \textbf{120 authentic conversational sentences} across Tamil, Telugu, and Hindi stratified across four duration tiers.
    \item \textbf{Full Model Integer Quantization}: Exported and verified the static INT8 ONNX pipeline, achieving a \textbf{74\% reduction in model size} (from 1.35\,GB FP32 down to 349\,MB) for the DiT backbone and quantizing the Vocos vocoder down to 22\,MB.
    \item \textbf{Analytical Memory Budget Formulation}: Established an analytical component memory budget of \textbf{801\,MB} for the proposed quantized IndicF5 pipeline (349\,MB DiT, 22\,MB Vocos, 280\,MB pre-allocated static arenas, 150\,MB runtime), demonstrating theoretical feasibility within 4\,GB physical RAM constraints.
    \item \textbf{Formulation of the Edge Acceleration Roadmap}: Established the post-midsem optimization roadmap incorporating Google LiteRT, trajectory step distillation (2-step and 4-step sampling), and hardware delegate acceleration for compatible silicon.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key Scientific, Systems, and Societal Contributions}
The technical and humanitarian contributions of this project span three primary dimensions:

\subsection{Theoretical and Linguistic Contributions}
By connecting continuous flow matching with the syllabic structure of Dravidian and Indo-Aryan scripts, this research provides an empirical characterization of akshara frame density for flow-matching TTS. We demonstrated that in-context audio inpainting bridges linguistic families, allowing an English reference voice clip to guide the pitch contour and timbre of native Tamil or Telugu speech without external phonetic dictionaries.

\subsection{Systems and Edge Deployment Contributions}
Through in-kernel physical memory profiling (\texttt{/proc/self/status}) across physical silicon tiers, this research demonstrated the operational viability and memory characteristics of mobile flow-matching speech synthesis. We showed that pairing static INT8 tensor graphs with Fourier-based Inverse STFT neural vocoders (Vocos) establishes an analytical component budget of 801\,MB, providing a viable blueprint for deterministic memory confinement on budget edge platforms.

\subsection{Pedagogical and Accessibility Intent in Untapped Domains}
Commercial speech synthesis platforms have predominantly focused on commercial assistants and global languages, leaving foundational education in vernacular public schools and special education for visually impaired learners relatively untapped. This project explores how on-device voice cloning addresses these societal opportunities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Familiar-Voice Learning for Government School Children}: Audio learning aids delivered in the familiar voice of a local classroom teacher have the potential to enhance listening engagement and emotional comfort for primary school children in regional environments.
    \item \textbf{Accessible Auditory Learning for Blind Students}: For visually impaired students who rely entirely on auditory channels for academic study, hearing textbook lessons and study materials articulated in their teacher's voice provides pedagogical continuity and cognitive familiarity.
    \item \textbf{Economic and Operational Feasibility}: Operating entirely offline on state-subsidized tablets (such as the Lenovo Tab K10) with zero recurring cloud API fees ensures that schools can generate educational audio without ongoing infrastructure costs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ethics, Biometric Privacy, and Responsible Deployment}
\label{sec:ethics_privacy}
Zero-shot voice cloning technology introduces sensitive ethical considerations, particularly when deployed in educational environments involving teachers and minors:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Informed Consent and Faculty Autonomy}: Voice capture and profile creation must require explicit, affirmative informed consent from teachers. The system must clearly explain how acoustic embeddings are extracted and restrict cloning strictly to approved instructional content.
    \item \textbf{Local Storage and Verifiable Deletion}: Teacher reference audio clips and acoustic catalogs must remain strictly on local application storage. The application architecture includes mechanisms to delete voice profiles permanently upon request, preventing unauthorized retention.
    \item \textbf{Mitigating Impersonation and Misuse Risks}: Operating voice cloning models on portable edge devices creates potential risks of unauthorized voice impersonation. Recommended security measures include local cryptographic protection for stored voice profiles, application-level authentication, and audible watermarking on generated educational audio.
    \item \textbf{Biometric Privacy through Offline Execution}: Rather than claiming absolute biometric security, the offline architecture provides a verifiable privacy boundary: because all tokenization, neural flow matching, and audio reconstruction execute entirely on-device, biometric voice features and teacher recordings are never transmitted to external cloud servers or third-party APIs.
    \item \textbf{Child Data Protection}: In compliance with digital child protection standards, the system is designed strictly for teacher-led instructional content delivery; no student voice data or personal identifiers are collected, processed, or stored.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Concluding Remarks and Immediate Priorities}
The work completed during this phase demonstrates that zero-shot multilingual voice cloning is architecturally feasible on budget mobile edge hardware, executing entirely offline without cloud dependencies. By tailoring this capability to vernacular government schools and accessible education for visually impaired students, this project establishes a practical foundation for personalized, familiar-voice audio learning. The engineering feasibility of this approach has been demonstrated through mathematical modeling, ten completed engineering iterations, and physical hardware profiling across four distinct silicon tiers.

The immediate priorities for the post-midsem phase are ordered as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Google LiteRT Migration}: Execute the \texttt{litert-torch} PyTorch-to-FlatBuffer conversion and validate numerical parity against the PyTorch FP32 baseline.
    \item \textbf{CPU and Accelerator Optimization}: Implement StableHLO operator fusion and evaluate delegate acceleration on compatible hardware (Qualcomm QNN HTP), while prioritizing ARM NEON CPU optimizations for the Helio P22T.
    \item \textbf{Sampling Step Distillation}: Apply trajectory distillation (4-step Reflow and 2-step FlashSpeech consistency models) to reduce the ODE integration steps, targeting interactive synthesis speeds on budget CPU hardware.
    \item \textbf{Quantized IndicF5 Hardware Benchmarking}: Deploy the native IndicF5 C++ engine onto target Android hardware (Lenovo Tab K10) to collect stage-level latency, resident memory, and thermal benchmarks under sustained operation.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Speech Quality and User Evaluation}: Conduct objective speech evaluations (WER, CER, speaker similarity) and structured user listening studies with educators and visually impaired students to assess pedagogical efficacy and listening comfort.
\end{enumerate}
